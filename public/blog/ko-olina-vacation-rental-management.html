Permits, Legal Requirements & AOAO Rules

The TVU Permit

Ko Olina's resort zoning makes properties eligible for Transient Vacation Unit (TVU) permits — but eligible is not the same as permitted. Each individual property must hold a valid TVU permit issued by the Honolulu Department of Planning and Permitting (DPP). Operating without one exposes owners to fines of up to $10,000 per day per the Revised Ordinances of Honolulu, Chapter 21.

To obtain a TVU permit:

  • Submit Form LU-1 (or the current equivalent) to DPP at 650 South King Street, 7th Floor, Honolulu
  • Include proof of ownership, property photos, and a floor plan showing the rental unit(s)
  • Pay the application fee (currently approximately $500–$1,000 depending on property type)
  • Allow 30–90 days for DPP review
  • Once issued, display the permit number at the property and on all listing platforms
  • Renew annually before expiration

Any reputable Ko Olina property manager will verify your TVU permit status before taking on a property. If a manager doesn't ask, that's a red flag.

AOAO Rules — The Variable That Trips Up New Owners

Ko Olina's resort zoning permits TVU activity at the city level, but condominium associations (AOAOs) layer their own rules on top. Even within Ko Olina, individual condo complexes can restrict or prohibit short-term rentals through their CC&Rs and House Rules.

Before assuming any Ko Olina condo is STR-eligible, review the complex's governing documents carefully. Ask specifically:

  • Does the CC&R prohibit rentals under 30 days?
  • Is there a minimum rental period in the House Rules?
  • Has there been any recent AOAO board action on STR policy?
  • Are there caps on the number of units that can be rented simultaneously?

Ko Olina Station, Ko Olina Beach Villas, and Ko Olina Kai are among the most STR-active complexes — but rules can change at annual AOAO meetings. Verify current status, not just historical status, before purchase or listing.

Seasonal Peaks in Ko Olina

Ko Olina's demand calendar differs slightly from East O'ahu markets like Kailua. Key peaks:

  • Christmas / New Year's (Dec 20 – Jan 4): Highest rates of the year. Lagoon-facing properties regularly achieve 2–3× base nightly rate. Book 6–9 months in advance.
  • Spring Break (mid-March through April): Family demand peaks. Ko Olina's calm lagoons and Disney Aulani proximity drive strong occupancy.
  • Summer (mid-June through August): Extended-stay family demand. Minimum stay rules of 5–7 nights make sense during this window.
  • October / November: Shoulder season. Target LPGA Lotte Championship attendees (held at Ko Olina Golf Club in spring), Iron Man World Championship overflow, and surf-season visitors.

How to Choose a Ko Olina Vacation Rental Manager

Not all Ko Olina property managers are equal. The market has attracted both dedicated specialists and large national chains that apply mainland frameworks to a market with Hawaii-specific legal and operational realities.

Questions to ask every prospective manager:

  1. "How many Ko Olina properties do you currently manage?" — Look for demonstrated local expertise, not just general O'ahu coverage.
  2. "What dynamic pricing tool do you use, and can you show me your methodology?" — Managers using Airbnb Smart Pricing are leaving your money on the table.
  3. "Who handles guest issues at 2am and what's the response protocol?" — The answer reveals actual operational infrastructure vs. a one-person operation.
  4. "Can I see an anonymized sample owner statement?" — Fee structures that look reasonable can hide owner-unfavorable practices in how expenses are handled.
  5. "Do you verify TVU permit status before taking on a property?" — Any manager who doesn't ask about permits upfront is an operational risk.
  6. "What's your average annual occupancy for managed Ko Olina properties?" — Get a number, not a vague assurance.
  7. "What's your management agreement notice period?" — You want to be able to exit without a lengthy lock-in if service quality declines.

Red flags: Managers who don't mention permits, can't give you actual occupancy numbers, use only Airbnb Smart Pricing, require 12-month lock-in agreements with no performance clauses, or can't show you a sample owner statement.

Tax Obligations for Ko Olina Owners

Hawaii has some of the highest vacation rental tax obligations in the country. Ko Olina owners need to understand these before their first booking:

Transient Accommodations Tax (TAT)

O'ahu's total TAT rate is 18.00% (state 10.25% + county 3% + O'ahu surcharge 4.75%). This applies to all short-term rental income. For platform bookings through Airbnb and VRBO, these platforms now remit TAT directly to the Hawaii Department of Taxation on behalf of hosts. For direct bookings, owners are responsible for collection and remittance.

General Excise Tax (GET)

O'ahu GET rate is 4.712% on gross rental income. As with TAT, Airbnb and VRBO remit GET on platform bookings. Direct bookings require owner remittance via the Hawaii Tax Online portal (G-45 form monthly or quarterly depending on annual volume).

Federal Income Tax

Rental income is reportable on Schedule E (or Schedule C if services are substantial). Hawaii has a state income tax rate up to 11%. Consult a Hawaii-licensed CPA before your first booking season — the combination of GET, TAT, state income tax, and federal income tax makes Ko Olina rental income a tax-complex matter that generic mainland CPAs often mishandle.

A professional Ko Olina property manager should provide end-of-year income summaries that separate platform-remitted taxes from owner-responsible amounts, simplifying your tax filing.
